# HRvLR figure audit and future directions

*A_HRvLR_2026 — skeletal-muscle DIA-MS proteomics, high vs low hypertrophy responders. Prepared 2026-07-09 on `feature/clustering-phenotype`.*

This document does three things. It records a tidy pass over stages 00–05 (Phase 0), it explains every published figure panel against the statistic that actually produces it (Phase 1), and it reads the exploratory `05_test` branch and proposes figures that would sharpen the responder question, each checked against the literature (Phase 2). The organising question throughout is the one the study was built to answer: do high and low responders differ in *how* the muscle proteome moves with training and with an acute bout, rather than only in where it sits at baseline.

A note on how to read the panel verdicts. Every claim below names the exact function, test, threshold, and seed that stands behind it, taken from the committed panel scripts rather than the prose. Where a panel's stated take-home reaches past what its statistic can carry, that is flagged in place. Those flags are not accusations of error; most of the figure notebooks are unusually candid about their own limits. They mark the spots a reviewer will press.

## Phase 0 — Tidy pass over stages 00–05

The pipeline is in good shape organizationally, so this was a light pass. Three checks came back clean. No render artifact is committed: every rendered `.html`, `_cache/`, and `_files/` is gitignored and only present as an untracked working file, and the tracked `.rds` files are the legitimate stage hand-offs the downstream scripts read. And there are no stray writes: every `write_csv`, `saveRDS`, `ggsave`, and `saveWorkbook` in stages 00–05 resolves to its own stage's `c_data/` or `b_reports/`, none to the repo root or a sibling stage.

The real findings were two single-source-of-truth violations, and both are fixed and pushed. F03's setup re-declared the full nine-contrast identity vector inline instead of deriving it from `03_DEP/contrasts.R`, so a rename in the canonical file would have silently desynchronised the figure; it now sources `contrasts.R` and derives the names with `sub(" =.*$", "", HRVLR_CONTRASTS)`. F03 was also re-run to confirm this empirically — its three tracked PNGs came back byte-identical (only the PDFs and the `.xlsx` re-timestamped, which is inherent to those formats and why the byte-identity target is CSV and PNG). The two changes were committed separately (`source the contrast set in f03…`, `use the shared pi_thresh in f06…`) and pushed.

A stale protein count in the top-level pipeline narrative (`1,885 × 45`, versus the real `1,896 × 45` in the committed matrix) was already corrected at HEAD by concurrent work on the branch during this pass, so it needed no action from me. Worth noting: the branch was under active development throughout — the working tree accumulated unrelated in-progress DEP-stage edits (`01_run_dep.R`, the proteoDA `static_plots`, `05_results.xlsx`) that are not mine, and I deliberately staged only my two figure-helper files so none of that concurrent work was swept into my commits.

## Phase 1 — The six figures and the imputation supplement

### F01 — Phenotype atlas

F01 answers the first question any responder study has to settle before it touches a mass spectrometer: are the two groups genuinely different phenotypes rather than two draws from one distribution. It reads a single file, `00_input/HRvLR_meta.csv`, and never touches the proteome. Every panel works from the categorical HR/LR grouping; the continuous composite score that *defines* that grouping appears only in Panel G. The statistical spine is a mixed ANOVA (`rstatix::anova_test` with `between = Group`, `within = Timepoint`, `wid = subject`) for the repeated-measures phenotypes, an unpaired Welch `t.test` for the single-timepoint measures, and paired within-group `t.test`s for the pre/post brackets. There is no `set.seed()`; jitter reproducibility is pinned by `seed = 42` inside each `position_jitter()`. The take-home is descriptive and it lands: the groups separate on growth outcomes while sharing the training that produced them.

Panel A (accumulated volume load) plots one value per subject at T3 and compares groups with an unpaired Welch `t.test(ACCUM_VL ~ Group)`. It exists to show the divergence is not a training-dose artifact — the point is that the groups did comparable work, so a null or near-null here is the desired result, not a failure. Panels B and H (leg-press and knee-extension 1RM) run the shared `prepost_delta_panel` builder: a mixed ANOVA for the group-by-time structure plus a between-group Welch test on the per-subject T2−T1 delta. Panels C, D, E (mixed, type I, and type II fibre cross-sectional area from manual tracing) and Panel I (whole-muscle CSA) use that same builder, so strength and size are all read on identical terms.

Panel F is the one structural inconsistency worth naming. It plots the MyoVision automated fibre-CSA measures as a cross-check on the manual tracing in C–E, and the notebook says the two sources are read "on comparable terms." In code they are not identical: the shared builder in C–E always draws its within-group and delta brackets with the raw p-value, whereas Panel F gates every bracket behind ANOVA significance *and* a paired/delta `p < 0.05` (`panel_F.R:80–95, 137–145`), and its audit CSV omits the p-value column that every other panel writes. The effect is cosmetic rather than statistical — the underlying tests match — but a non-significant contrast that C–E would print as a number is silently blank in F. If the manuscript claims the manual and automated pipelines are shown identically, that claim is slightly ahead of the code.

Panel G is the composite hypertrophy score, plotted by group with no significance test on purpose: the groups are *defined* by this score, so a between-group p-value would be circular. The notebook states this plainly and the code honours it (the subtitle is hard-coded "Groups defined by this composite"). This is the correct call. The only loose thread across F01 is the composite figure caption, which reads "n = 8 per group" while the panels drop `NA` rows and print realised per-group n dynamically, so an individual bar can rest on fewer than eight subjects — a stated-versus-computed n mismatch, not a test error.

### F02 — Global proteome overview and quality control

F02 is the orientation layer: before any contrast-specific biology, it asks whether the proteome's variance structure matches the design, whether the two groups are equally noisy, and how much signal survives testing. It reads the normalized non-imputed matrix, the missForest-imputed matrix (for PCA and CV), the stage-03 combined results, and the metadata. It works on the 7-contrast set (dropping the two purely-descriptive between-group timepoint contrasts) and narrows to 5 for most panels by also dropping the interactions. Seeds are `set.seed(42)` before the PERMANOVA (Panel A) and before the fgsea loop (Panel H).

Panel A is the design-geometry check: `prcomp` on the imputed matrix, drawn twice (coloured by group, then by timepoint) with 80% `stat_ellipse`, annotated with two `vegan::adonis2` PERMANOVAs at 999 permutations. The two tests are deliberately built on different units — timepoint is tested with `strata = Subject_ID` restricted permutation, group is tested on subject-collapsed mean profiles so the unbalanced partial subjects cannot act as unequal blocks. This is a careful, defensible construction; the only subtlety is that the two annotated p-values are therefore not computed on the same rows the points represent, which the notebook discloses. Panel C (per-protein CV) computes coefficient of variation on the *linear* scale (`2^imp_mat`), HR against LR within each timepoint, with a Pearson correlation and an analytic Fisher-z interval (`fisher_z_ci`). Computing CV on linear intensities rather than log values is the correct convention, though the panel attributes it to a "Brenes 2024" reference that a Crossref and OpenAlex search could not locate — the closest verifiable Brenes work is the 2019 multi-batch TMT precision study (Molecular & Cellular Proteomics, doi:10.1074/mcp.RA119.001472), which is a TMT paper rather than a DIA CV-on-linear-scale source, so that citation should be re-checked before the manuscript ships. Panel M reads a precomputed per-protein eta-squared (variance explained by `Group_Time`) from a stage-02 intermediate and shows its distribution; it is an orientation histogram, and its one hard number — the count of proteins above eta-squared 0.5 — depends entirely on that arbitrary 0.5 cut.

The biological panels are B, F, H, I, J, and the supplementary G. Panel B shows signed-logFC histograms per contrast with a median-absolute-logFC annotation and no significance filter — deliberately, as the effect-size context for reading the counts that follow. Panel F is the DEP-count bar chart, and it is where a reader must be careful: the light bars count nominal `P.Value < 0.05` (unadjusted), while the dark bars and the tip labels count the pi-score flag (`sig_pi != 0`). Two significance rules share one plot, and the "how much signal is there" take-home depends on which one the eye lands on. Panel H is the only FDR-controlled panel in the figure: `fgsea` on moderated-t ranks against MSigDB C5 GO subsets, `minSize = 10, maxSize = 500`, significance at `padj < 0.05`. Panels I and J are three-set Euler diagrams (`eulerr`) of pi-gated training and acute responder sets with a companion direction bar; the overlaps read as shared response and the private regions as responder-specific, all gated on the categorical pi flag. Panel G, in the supplement, is the many-set `UpSet` counterpart.

Two labelling issues run through F02. The `CONTRAST_COLORS` palette defines nine contrasts but the figure only ever uses seven (and mostly five), so `Trained_HRvLR` and `Acute_HRvLR` carry colours nothing plots. And several subtitles read "Pi < 0.05" while the code never applies a 0.05 cut to a pi value — it reads the categorical `sig_pi ∈ {−1,0,1}` flag written in stage 03. The number is asserted in text, computed upstream. Neither changes a result; both are worth tightening before the figure ships.

### F03 — enrichVolcano ring-volcanoes

F03 is the "what moves where" figure and the compute hub for F04/F05. For each of the nine contrasts it draws a volcano at the centre and a ring of enriched-pathway arcs around it, so one panel answers two questions at once: which proteins move, and which pathways those movements add up to. It reads only `03_combined_results.csv`. The enrichment is `fgsea::fgseaMultilevel` (via `run_fgsea_deduplicated`) ranked on the limma **moderated-t statistic**, not the pi-score, with `nPermSimple = 10000`, `eps = 0`, `minSize = 15`, `maxSize = 500`, seeded `set.seed(42)`. Gene sets come from `build_pathway_collection`: MSigDB Hallmark, Reactome (`CP:REACTOME`), GO:BP, a curated GO-Slim, and — worth stating precisely — KEGG as the `CP:KEGG_MEDICUS` subcollection, not legacy KEGG. Ring arcs are filtered at genuine `padj < 0.05` and capped at the top seven per direction. The whole enrichment table is written to `F03/c_data/F03_source_data.xlsx`, fresh every run, and that workbook is what F04 and F05 read.

The single panel-level subtlety is a naming trap the notebook itself documents: `enrichVolcano` labels its point-significance column `padj`, but the value handed in is the pi-score (`P.Value^|logFC|`) gated at `< 0.05`. So two significance rules coexist inside one figure — a pi-gate colours the volcano points, real BH-FDR gates the pathway arcs. Nothing is wrong here, but anyone reading the code cold will misread that `padj` slot. The take-home — that the differential and interaction contrasts carry the responder story while the within-group contrasts carry the plainer "what changed" story — is a framing statement rather than a tested claim, and F03 is honest that the tested version lives downstream in F04/F05.

### F04 and F05 — Training-phase and acute-phase concordance

These two figures carry the analytic core. F04 asks whether HR and LR move the same biology the same way across the training phase (T1→T2), F05 asks the same for the acute bout (T2→T3), and both are rendered by one parameterised driver (`render_concordance_figure`), so any critique of one applies to the other. Neither recomputes enrichment; both read F03's cached `fgsea_all` sheet. Panel A correlates HR and LR *protein* logFC with a Spearman rho and classifies each protein into shared/divergent quadrants, where "divergent" is defined by the interaction pi-flag rather than by the scatter. Panel B correlates HR and LR *pathway* NES (Hallmark + GO-Slim) with a Spearman `cor.test`. The supplements add a `fry` rotation test on the imputed matrix (the only fresh limma computation in these figures), an `RRHO2` rank-rank map, a threshold-sensitivity table, and a percentile bootstrap of the rho.

This is where the take-home outruns the statistic, and it is worth being exact because the figures are the manuscript's spine. The stated conclusion is "shared adaptation and responder-specific divergence." The protein-level Spearman rho that anchors it is **0.198** for F04 (95% bootstrap CI [0.151, 0.249]) and **0.152** for F05 (CI [0.108, 0.199]). Both intervals exclude zero, but the magnitude is weak, and — decisively — the bootstrap resamples *proteins*, not subjects, so the roughly two-thousand-protein n drives an interval far tighter than the 8-versus-8 subject design can honestly support. The notebooks disclose this ("the rho says how aligned the two acute profiles are, no more"), but a reader who takes the CI at face value will over-read it. Three internal cross-checks complicate the "shared" reading further. The pathway-level NES concordance (Panel B) is visibly *strong* while the protein-level rho is weak, so the figure's two concordance measures disagree in magnitude. The `fry` rotation test — the proper set-level check of whether HR's DEPs move together along LR's ranking — is non-significant in both figures (F04: up p = 0.560, down p = 0.246; F05: down p = 0.087, up p = 0.267), so the directional shared-response claim has no support from the one test built to confirm it. And the threshold-sensitivity table shows the concordant-versus-discordant balance flips with the significance rule: at strict FDR almost nothing survives, at nominal p the discordant proteins nearly match the concordant. The honest framing, which the figures nearly reach, is that pathway-level responses are broadly shared while protein-level concordance is weak and threshold-dependent, and that the interaction contrast — not the scatter — is what licenses any divergence claim.

### F06 — WGCNA module–phenotype linkage

F06 asks which co-expression modules track the adaptation. It runs WGCNA on the missForest-imputed proteome and links module eigengenes to the continuous hypertrophy phenotype. The network is built with `blockwiseModules`, `networkType = "signed"`, power chosen as the first value clearing a signed-R² of 0.87 (Horvath's sample-size fallback of 12 otherwise), `minModuleSize = 30`, `mergeCutHeight = 0.25`, `deepSplit = 2`, determinism via `randomSeed = 12345` (WGCNA's own argument; there is no `set.seed`). Correlation type is the WGCNA default Pearson, not bicor. The phenotype linkage comes in three read-outs: a baseline-T1 prediction with leave-one-out Q² and Pearson `cor.test` against seven adaptation traits (n ≤ 16 subjects), a per-timepoint point-biserial HR/LR responder correlation, and a Spearman correlation of the T1→T3 eigengene change against the continuous traits (Panel C). Panel S adds a mixed model, `lmer(ME ~ trait * timepoint + (1 | subject))` via `lmerTest`, testing the trait-by-timepoint interaction with Satterthwaite F-tests and BH correction within trait.

Two things must be said plainly. First, the effective sample size is inconsistent between the network and the linkage: the network is constructed on 45 subject-by-timepoint columns treated as independent, while the trait associations correctly collapse to 16 subjects (or ~15 per timepoint). Treating a subject's three timepoints as three independent samples to hit WGCNA's sample-size guidance, then treating them as one subject for the trait test, is a tension the code comment acknowledges for the linkage but not for the network build. Second — and this is the figure's genuine strength — the phenotype read-outs are honest nulls. The baseline module eigengenes do not predict hypertrophy (leave-one-out Q² is not positive), no module–trait interaction clears FDR in Panel S, and the notebook states each null as a null rather than dressing a nominal hit as a finding. The one place the take-home is safe is exactly the place the statistic is weakest, because the take-home is "nothing survives." That matches the recorded memory that the baseline/training proteome cannot predict hypertrophy and only the acute proteome carries suggestive structure. The object is still named `module_prediction` while the figure reframes it as association; the notebook flags this itself.

### S_imputation — imputation-method supplement

The supplement asks whether the reported effects depend on the imputation choice. It compares the non-imputed reference against the four arms (missForest, imp4p, MsCoreUtils hybrid, Perseus MNAR) on two axes: a per-contrast Spearman rho of each arm's logFC against the non-imputed fit (Panel A heatmap), and a count of pi-significant proteins per arm (Panel B). missForest is the canonical downstream matrix, asserted upstream (`CANONICAL <- "missforest"`) rather than selected by this comparison. The stated take-home is that "the key effects hold across imputation choice."

That take-home overstates one of the four arms it plots. The missForest, imp4p, and MsCoreUtils arms do track the non-imputed fit well (rho roughly 0.84–0.98), but the Perseus MNAR arm does not: on the key contrasts its Spearman rho falls to 0.36–0.57. The effects do *not* hold under Perseus, so "across imputation choice" is contradicted by the arm designed to be the most aggressive about missing-not-at-random values. The colour floor in Panel A (`limits = c(min(0.9, min(rho)), 1)`) is pulled down to Perseus's own minimum, which visually softens exactly that outlier; the numeric labels are the only unambiguous signal. Two narrower points: the comparison is rank-only (Spearman on logFC), so it speaks to monotone agreement, not magnitude agreement or agreement in *which* proteins pass significance; and the "missForest is canonical" statement is a fixed upstream decision, not a result this figure derives. The clean framing is that the three MAR-style arms agree with the non-imputed reference and missForest agrees best, while the Perseus MNAR draw diverges — which is itself a useful, publishable point about MNAR handling, but it is the opposite of "the choice doesn't matter."

## Phase 2 — 05_test and future figures

### What 05_test does

`05_test` is an exploratory branch that asks the responder question at the level of pathway *activity* rather than individual proteins. It scores every sample against a gene-set collection with GSVA (and, in the multi-database script, ssGSEA as well), turning the protein matrix into a pathway-by-sample score matrix, and then feeds those per-sample scores into linear mixed models. Three model shapes appear. The trajectory model (`02_lmm_trajectory.R`) fits `lmer(score ~ group * timepoint + (1 | subject))` per pathway and reads off the group-by-time interaction — the pathway-level version of the responder question. The phenotype model (`03_lmm_phenotype.R`) swaps the categorical group for a continuous hypertrophy trait, `score ~ trait * timepoint + (1 | subject)`, which is the more defensible framing. And a baseline-prediction analysis asks whether the T1 pathway scores predict the eventual hypertrophy trait out of sample, using leave-one-out cross-validation with a max-over-pathways permutation null. The gene sets are MSigDB Hallmark plus a GO-Slim for the core scripts, expanded to Hallmark, KEGG_MEDICUS, Reactome, GO:BP, and GO-Slim in the multi-database comparison. Every model is seeded `set.seed(42)`, and the README is unusually frank about the method's soft spots.

### Where it is honest, and where it overreaches

The branch is honest in three important ways. It uses the continuous phenotype, not only the HR/LR split, in the phenotype model — the right instinct, because dichotomising a continuous response into responders and non-responders throws away information and manufactures apparent group differences that are partly regression to the mean (Atkinson & Batterham 2015; Swinton et al. 2018). It applies BH correction across pathways within each model. And for the prediction analysis it references the selected best pathway to a permutation null that itself takes the maximum over all pathways, which is the correct way to price in "best of many" selection rather than pretending the top hit was pre-specified.

It overreaches, or at least invites overreach, in four places, and they compound. First, the effective sample size is small and should be stated as such: the mixed models run on 45 subject-by-timepoint observations but only 16 subjects as random-intercept levels, which is near the floor for stable variance-component estimation even with the Satterthwaite degrees of freedom that `lmerTest` supplies. Second, the GSVA scores are cohort-relative by construction — the kernel estimates each sample's enrichment against the empirical distribution of the whole 45-sample cohort, so a score is not a property of a sample but of a sample within this particular set of samples, and it does not transport to a new subject unchanged (Foroutan, Bhuva et al. 2018). Third, the headline pathway lists in the trajectory and multi-database figures are selected by *nominal* interaction p-value, with FDR shown only as a flag, so "the pathways with the strongest divergence" is a ranked display of uncorrected hits. The README concedes this.

The fourth issue is the serious one, and it is a train/test leak. The leave-one-out prediction re-fits only the downstream `lm(trait ~ score)` inside each fold; the GSVA scores themselves are computed once on the full 45-sample cohort (`01_gsva_scores.R`) and then read back and sliced. Because GSVA is cohort-relative, the held-out subject's baseline score was estimated using that subject's own data together with everyone else's, so information from the test point has already leaked into its own predictor before the fold is drawn. The honest procedure — re-running GSVA on only the training subjects inside each fold — is described in the README as what a "strictly honest LOO would" do, which is an accurate way of saying the implemented version does not. The permutation null shuffles the phenotype but re-uses the same leaked scores, so it controls for pathway multiplicity but not for the scoring leak, and the reported Q² values are optimistically biased even before selection. This does not sink the exploration; it means the prediction numbers cannot be read as out-of-sample performance, and the branch should either say so in one sentence or fix the fold. Finally, pairing single-sample enrichment scores with linear mixed models has, as far as a targeted PubMed and OpenAlex search could find, no published precedent in exercise or skeletal-muscle omics — which is not disqualifying, but it means the method travels as a composition of validated parts (GSVA; mixed models) framed as exploratory, not as an established design with a citation behind it.

### Future figures

The two places this dataset is strongest are the acute contrasts and the continuous hypertrophy phenotype, and the memory of prior runs agrees: the baseline and trained proteome do not predict hypertrophy, while the acute proteome carries the only suggestive structure. The proposals below aim there. Each is designed to be built as its own `F0x` scaffold under the house style — `a_script/` with a narrative `.qmd`, `b_reports/`, `c_data/`; the nine contrasts and `PI_THRESH` sourced from `03_DEP/contrasts.R`, the theme from `04_Figures/functions/style.R`, PCA from `shared/pca.R`; `here::here()` paths, `set.seed(42)` at each stochastic call site, canonical packages over hand-rolled code.

**Proposal 1 — The acute response as a continuous gradient, not a dichotomy.** The question is whether the magnitude of the acute proteome response (T3 − T2) scales with the continuous hypertrophy phenotype across all sixteen subjects, which is a stronger and less arbitrary test than comparing two bins. The method is a single limma fit that replaces the categorical responder factor with the continuous phenotype interacted with timepoint — a design along the lines of `~ 0 + time + time:pheno` with `duplicateCorrelation` blocking on subject — and reads the acute slope, the coefficient for how each protein's T2→T3 change moves with phenotype. The central panel is a volcano of that slope: x is the per-protein phenotype-by-acute-change coefficient, y is −log10 of its moderated-t p-value, coloured by the pi-gate, with an fgsea ring or leading-edge callout for the acute-response pathways so the protein-level and pathway-level readings sit together. A companion panel orders the sixteen subjects along the phenotype axis and shows, as a heatmap, the per-subject acute delta of the leading-edge proteins, so a monotone left-to-right gradient is visible directly. A positive result is a set of nonzero, consistent-sign slopes concentrated in coherent acute-response pathways (the mitochondrial, stress-signalling, and translational programmes that the acute-exercise phosphoproteome literature would predict; Hoffman et al. 2015); a null is slopes scattered symmetrically around zero with no pathway coherence and nothing surviving FDR. The assumptions are that the phenotype effect is approximately linear over its range and that the composite phenotype is measured with modest error; both are reasonable and both are stated rather than hidden. This figure uses the two strongest axes at once and sidesteps the dichotomisation critique entirely.

**Proposal 2 — The acute interaction, with a subject-honest interval.** F05 already asks whether HR and LR diverge acutely, but its confidence rests on a protein-resampling bootstrap that treats roughly two thousand proteins as the sample size, which is why the interval around a Spearman rho of 0.15 looks reassuringly tight while the fry rotation test — the proper set-level check — is non-significant. The sharper figure keeps the acute-interaction question but fixes the inference: resample *subjects* (the eight HR and eight LR), not proteins, so the interval reflects the design that actually generated the data, and lead with the modelled `Acute_Interaction` contrast rather than the eyeball concordance. The panels are a NES-versus-NES scatter of HR against LR acute pathway scores with a subject-bootstrap confidence band, an RRHO2 map for the threshold-free rank overlap, and a forest of the FDR-controlled interaction pathways with effect sizes. A positive result is one or more acute pathways whose interaction NES clears FDR with a subject-bootstrap interval excluding zero; a null — the more likely outcome given the current fry results — is that no pathway survives once the interval is honest, which is itself a clean, reportable statement that the acute divergence is weaker than the protein-resampled view suggested. The assumption is that eight subjects per arm can support a rotation test at all, which `fry` with `duplicateCorrelation` is built to handle at small n; the figure should show the null honestly rather than lowering the threshold until something appears.

**Proposal 3 — Leakage-corrected prediction from the acute proteome.** This turns 05_test's most interesting and most flawed analysis into a defensible one. The question is whether the acute proteome predicts the continuous hypertrophy phenotype out of sample, and the fix is to make the per-sample scoring transport across folds. Two clean routes exist: replace cohort-relative GSVA with a rank-based single-sample score that is computed independently per sample and therefore does not leak (singscore; Foroutan, Bhuva et al. 2018), or keep GSVA but re-estimate it inside each leave-one-out fold on the training subjects only, which is the procedure the current README describes but does not run. The figure contrasts baseline-only prediction against acute-informed prediction of the phenotype, panel by panel: predicted-versus-observed scatter per fold, the leave-one-out Q² for each pathway against the max-over-pathways permutation null, and a direct baseline-versus-acute comparison of predictive gain. A positive result is an acute-informed Q² that is greater than zero and beats the selection-adjusted null under genuinely nested cross-validation; a null — consistent with the recorded prior finding — is Q² at or below zero, which would confirm rigorously that even the acute proteome does not carry out-of-sample predictive signal at this sample size. Either outcome is publishable, because the value is in the honest cross-validation, not in a positive hit; the assumption is only that the phenotype is fixed per subject, which it is. Framed this way, the analysis that currently overstates becomes the one that most cleanly answers whether prediction is possible at all.

**Proposal 4 — Phenotype-by-time trajectories at the pathway level, corrected.** The phenotype mixed model in 05_test is the right idea reported the wrong way, so a compact figure would keep `lmer(score ~ pheno * timepoint + (1 | subject))` but focus on the acute slope (T2→T3), rank pathways by their FDR-adjusted interaction rather than nominal p, and plot effect sizes with intervals instead of a top-six-by-p list. Precedent exists for regressing co-expression module or pathway summaries on the magnitude of the training response in muscle omics (O'Bryan et al. 2024), and set-level analysis is exactly where small-n studies recover reproducible signal that single-protein tests miss (Subramanian et al. 2005). A positive result is a pathway whose phenotype-by-acute-time interaction survives FDR with a credible effect size; a null is a flat field of small, non-significant slopes. The only change from the existing script is discipline in selection and reporting, so this is the cheapest of the four to build and the one that most directly rehabilitates work already done.

A closing note on novelty and framing. The responder-versus-non-responder muscle transcriptomics tradition (Davidsen et al. 2011; the Bamman-group cluster analyses) and the resistance-training muscle proteomics literature (Deshmukh et al. 2021; Hulmi et al. 2025) give strong precedent for the design as a whole, and WGCNA in exercising muscle and GSEA robustness at small n are both well established. What has essentially no direct precedent is the specific claim these figures chase — that the *acute* proteome, one hour post-bout, diverges by hypertrophy-response magnitude — because the acute-exercise proteome and phosphoproteome papers do not stratify by responder class, and the responder papers are baseline or chronic. That gap is the study's most original angle, and it should be framed as a hypothesis the acute contrasts are powered to *suggest* rather than confirm, with the continuous phenotype carrying the argument and the dichotomy kept only for display.
